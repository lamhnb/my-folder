# Geometry reconstruction boundary

Frames inspected: Phase 2 native pilot 1098.030267 and 1098.063633 (front oblique moving bowl), #01375 1070.002267 (rim clipped, enlarged hub), #01375 1113.512400 and #01376 1064.029633 (collector closeups), plus pilot setup overview 1053.062–1057.667. Original frames remain in earlier phases/local input; this folder redistributes no new third-party pixels.

Additional native frames inspected from all remaining broadcasts: #01373 1096.061633 (rim clipped at top/bottom; no full ellipse fit), #01374 1088.053633 (green balls; changed framing) and #01376 1029.028000 (front-oblique full bowl). Approximate hub centers/rim edges are appended to the landmark ledger, with missing/clipped geometry left unfilled. These confirm camera scale/center differences already flagged in Phase 2; they do not identify a machine or ball-set change. Historical Phase 1 imagery and Phase 3 settled-ball evidence supply event/identity context, not independent metric views for 3D triangulation.

`landmarks.csv` contains approximate manual image coordinates with conservative click ranges, **not calibrated physical measurements**. Pilot rim width ~841 px, height ~875 px; projected hub width ~244 px, about 0.290 rim width. Hub center differs from rim center by ~73 px horizontally. Perspective, refraction, camera angle and hub depth can all produce this offset; it does not establish eccentric shaft location. Fixed rim screws and housing corners offer future tracking landmarks; blurred transparent paddles do not supply fixed material landmarks.

Circularizing the projected rim with x'=(x-859.5)/420.5, y'=(y-549.5)/437.5 is an image normalization only. A single ellipse cannot identify intrinsics, camera pose, true rim shape, refractive interface, depth or metric scale. No metric rectification is claimed. Collector front corners are not assumed to lie in the shaft or bowl plane. Closeups have different cameras/scales; no triangulation across cuts is justified.

The nominal 50.5 mm historical ball diameter is **not** used as an image ruler: the actual 2026 set is unlinked, balls sit at unknown depths, and moving contours blur. Absolute bowl diameter/depth, wall thickness, paddle curvature/clearances, axis placement and the hidden gate remain unidentifiable.

`chamber.obj` is a collision-capable spherical-shell hypothesis, parametrized in metres by `simulator/geometry.py`. Inner radius 0.32 m and scale 1.0 are assumptions. Outer shell thickness 8 mm is assumed. Three curved box-chain paddles on one horizontal y axis, at 120 degrees, are an unverified topology. The front-view paddle clearance is radius minus arm length/section envelope; depth clearance also depends on the spherical wall. The bottom opening, two-gate pocket, sloping rail and special keeper are hypothetical alternatives to unknown actual internals. All collide in 3D; render uses the same dimensions. No CAD identity or 1:1 claim.

Future recovery needs dimensioned CAD or reference targets in synchronized calibrated multiview frames, camera/refraction calibration, and permitted slow-operation footage of the collector and loading port.

Loading port dimensions/topology are unknown and no verified loading-port mesh is supplied. The two loading configurations sample 55 nonoverlapping initial sphere positions inside the cavity (random volume or upper layer), excluding the initial paddle volume. They do not simulate a recovered sequential loading trajectory from a real rack. This missing mechanism is an explicit fidelity limit rather than a visually plausible invented CAD feature.

The visible central hub/shaft housing is used as an image landmark but has no independently identified 3D solid collision volume in this implementation. Paddles begin at an assumed 35 mm root radius; the central volume is therefore an unvalidated simplification. Hub thickness, shaft protrusion and per-ball CoM/shape anisotropy require measured geometry before a calibrated variant can replace this assumption. Their potential impact on mixing/selection remains unresolved.

The assumed result trough is 92 mm wide and can pack the six main spheres in a staggered pair of rows. This is a physical retention testbed, not a measured reconstruction of the broadcast's display layout or individual numbered keepers. That downstream layout fidelity is unresolved even when collection/conservation tests pass.
